2026 Prospect Ranks

Chance of MLB success from 2026 Low-A through Triple-A stats

The chance each 2026 Low-A, High-A, AA, and AAA hitter (under 130 career MLB at-bats through 2026) becomes a 10+ career WAR player, has a 3+ WAR season, reaches 20+ career WAR, has a 5+ WAR season, or, judging the bat alone, has a 125+ wRC+ season (400+ PA).
Level
How it works: the model is trained on minor-league hitters at Low-A and above from 2007–2019 and their MLB WAR through the age-27 season (2020 WAR is scaled up to a full season), though the probabilities shown are for full careers. It is a graded logistic regression: rather than fit each outcome separately, it learns one ladder of results (under 1, 1–3, 3–5, 5–10, or 10+ WAR), so rare outcomes like 20+ WAR also learn from the players who came close. To turn its scores into career probabilities, the scores for players the model wasn't trained on are matched to what the 2007–2014 players did over their whole careers to date (2007–2018 for the single-season outcomes). So a player shown at 20% looks like players who really did get there about 1 time in 5.
Outcomes: FanGraphs WAR for position players (batting, baserunning, fielding, and position). The 125+ wRC+ column judges the bat alone: a big-league season with a 125+ wRC+ in 400+ PA.
Stats used: the current season and the one before it, each counted separately. Every stat is compared with the player's league that season, and K%, BB%, ISO, and wOBA are also adjusted for park. Each stat is also pulled toward league average according to how many PA it takes to stabilize: strikeout rate is trusted quickly, wOBA only slowly, so a hot 150-PA line counts for less than the same line over a full season. A player at two levels gets a PA-weighted blend. The model also uses a combined estimate of each skill from both seasons, with the earlier season counted at about 70% and moved forward a year for the typical development of a hitter his age (and for any promotion).
Major factors (how much each multiplies the odds of 10+ WAR, all else equal): age vs. league (by birthdate), ×2.2 per year younger; wOBA, ×1.6 per standard deviation; ISO, ×1.3 per SD; Speed Score, ×1.3 per SD; K%, ×1.3 per SD fewer strikeouts; position, for a full season at C ×4, SS ×3.3, CF ×2.8, 3B ×2.1, 2B ×1.2 (compared with 1B, DH, LF, or RF); share of games started, ×1.9 per 10 points more; prior pro seasons, ×1.25 for each fewer season it took to reach the same age vs. league. The last two reflect what his organization thinks of him: a hitter who isn't starting every day, or who needed more seasons to get where he is, tends to do worse than his stats alone suggest.
Minor factors: BB%; swinging-strike, pull, and ground-ball/line-drive/fly-ball rates; level; and prior MLB at-bats.
Accuracy: on players it wasn't trained on, the model's AUC (1 = perfect ranking, 0.5 = a coin flip) is .90 for 10+ WAR, .90 for a 3+ WAR season, .93 for 20+ WAR, and .94 for a 5+ WAR season. Ranking the 2015–2018 classes this way, its top picks did about as well as the hitters on MLB Pipeline's Top 100 the following winter. One caveat: a player who DHed because of an injury is treated like a DH.
Who's covered: hitters with 150+ PA in 2026 at Low-A or higher and fewer than 130 career MLB at-bats through 2026. A hitter who missed most of 2026 (usually because of injury) is included if he had 150+ PA at a level in 2025 (Stats = “2025”). His chances start from his 2025 season, are lowered to reflect how hitters who missed most of a season have historically fared, and are adjusted for how he hit in his limited 2026 PA, but never end up higher than his 2025 season alone would give. Arizona Fall League and other off-season stats are not used.
